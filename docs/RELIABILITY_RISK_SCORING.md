## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

In Day 9, **PerceptGuard AI** implemented and audited an end-to-end recurrent forecasting pipeline (`LSTMForecaster`) that predicts continuous detection reliability scores ($\hat{R}_{t+H} \in [0.0, 1.0]$) across future horizons $H \in \{1, 3, 5\}$ video frames ahead.

The objective of the **Reliability Risk Scoring Engine** (`src/risk/`) is to transform those raw forecasting outputs into mathematically defensible, bounded, and traceable **perception failure risk scores**. This provides the quantitative foundation for subsequent risk classification (Task 3) and actionable driver alerting (Task 4).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Scientific Clarity on Risk vs. Calibrated Probability:**  
> The derived risk scores generated by this engine reflect the inverse expectation of object detection reliability ($1.0 - \hat{R}$). They represent an interpretable, monotonic indicator of perception degradation. They are **NOT** statistically calibrated failure probabilities (e.g. via Platt scaling or isotonic regression); calibration against empirical validation failure distributions belongs to downstream tasks.

## 2. Reliability-to-Risk Transformation

### 2.1 Monotonic Inverse Mapping
In autonomous perception, reliability $\hat{R} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ measures tracking and detection fidelity (e.g. bounding box IoU $\ge 0.50$):
- $\hat{R} = 1.0$: Perfect perception success (all objects detected and localized).
- $\hat{R} = 0.0$: Total perception failure (zero ground-truth objects detected).

Failure risk $S \in [0.0, 1.0]$ measures the degree of impending perception hazard:
- $S = 0.0$: Minimum perception failure risk (nominal driving conditions).
- $S = 1.0$: Maximum perception failure risk (complete detection blackout).

The fundamental transformation is defined as:
$$S(t+H) = 1.0 - \hat{R}_{t+H}$$

### 2.2 Numerical Safety & Conformance Rules
1. **Input Range Invariant:** Any input $\hat{R} \notin [0.0, 1.0]$ raises a `RiskValidationError`.
2. **Non-Finite Invariant:** Any $\text{NaN}$ or $\pm\infty$ input fails fast with a `RiskValidationError`.
3. **Conservation Invariant:** For all valid predictions:
   $$S(t+H) + \hat{R}_{t+H} = 1.0 \pm 10^{-6}$$
4. **Precision Conformance:** Output scores are rounded to configurable decimal places (default: 6 decimals).

## 3. Multi-Horizon Risk Evaluation ($H1, H3, H5$)

The risk scoring engine evaluates risk independently across the exact forward horizons established in Day 8 and Day 9:
- **$H1$ Risk ($t+1$ frame ahead):** Evaluates immediate next-frame detection failure risk.
- **$H3$ Risk ($t+3$ frames ahead):** Evaluates short-term failure risk (~100 ms lead time at 30 FPS).
- **$H5$ Risk ($t+5$ frames ahead):** Evaluates mid-term failure risk (~167 ms lead time at 30 FPS).

Every output record (`HorizonRiskRecord`) preserves:
- `prediction_id`: Unique prediction key (e.g. `{sequence_id}_H{horizon}`).
- `sequence_id`: Unique temporal sequence identifier.
- `source_frame_id`: Final observed frame filename.
- `target_frame_id`: Future forecasted frame filename.
- `target_timestamp_seconds`: Physical presentation timestamp.
- `horizon`: Integer horizon in $\{1, 3, 5\}$.
- `predicted_reliability`: Original model output.
- `risk_score`: Derived failure risk score.
- `model_name` & `checkpoint_id`: Provenance metadata.

## 4. Retrospective Temporal Risk Features

To support subsequent risk classification and alerting, the engine extracts temporal features using **strictly retrospective observations** up to time $t$:

```
Observed Past History [t-4, ..., t] ──► Feature Extractor ──► Temporal Risk Features
                                                                   │
                                                                   ├── Cross-Horizon Spread
                                                                   ├── Monotonic Degradation
                                                                   ├── Degradation Rate
                                                                   ├── Historical Trend Slope
                                                                   └── Recent Volatility
```

### Extracted Feature Definitions:
1. **Cross-Horizon Spread ($\Delta R_{1 \to 5}$):**
   $$\Delta R_{1 \to 5} = \hat{R}_{t+1} - \hat{R}_{t+5}$$
   A large positive spread indicates that perception is expected to degrade rapidly as the forecast horizon extends.
2. **Monotonic Degradation Flag:**
   $$\mathbb{I}_{\text{monotonic}} = \begin{cases} \text{True} & \text{if } \hat{R}_{t+1} \ge \hat{R}_{t+3} \ge \hat{R}_{t+5} \\ \text{False} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
3. **Degradation Rate ($d\hat{R}/dH$):**
   $$\text{Rate} = \frac{\hat{R}_{t+1} - \hat{R}_{t+5}}{4}$$
4. **Historical Trend Slope ($d\hat{R}/dt$):**
   Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) slope computed across past sequence reliability scores over a window of $W=5$ history steps. Negative slope indicates deteriorating perception prior to prediction time.
5. **Recent Prediction Volatility:**
   Standard deviation of historical reliability scores:
   $$\sigma_{\text{recent}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k=1}^K (\hat{R}_k - \bar{R})^2}$$

## 5. Risk Score Aggregation Strategies

The engine supports four configurable multi-horizon aggregation strategies:

| Strategy | Mathematical Formulation | Best Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`weighted_linear`** *(Default)* | $S_{\text{agg}} = \frac{\sum_{h \in \mathcal{H}} w_h S_h}{\sum_{h \in \mathcal{H}} w_h}$ | Balanced synthesis prioritizing near-term horizons ($w_1=0.50, w_3=0.35, w_5=0.15$). |
| **`worst_case`** | $S_{\text{agg}} = \max_{h \in \mathcal{H}}(S_h)$ | Safety-critical conservative mode; triggers if any horizon predicts failure. |
| **`primary_horizon`** | $S_{\text{agg}} = S_{h_{\text{primary}}}$ | Dedicated focus on a single horizon (e.g. $H=3$). |
| **`none`** | Returns unweighted individual horizon scores. | Telemetry inspection without synthesis. |

### Dynamic Weight Renormalization:
When a horizon is missing near video boundaries (e.g. sequence at frame 298 where $t+5 > 300$), the engine dynamically renormalizes available weights:
$$w_i' = \frac{w_i}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{available}}} w_j}$$
This ensures the aggregated score remains strictly bounded in $[0.0, 1.0]$ without artificially defaulting missing values to zero.
